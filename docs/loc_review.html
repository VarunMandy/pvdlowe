Two builders for the same record

pvdlowe/screening/candidates.py

84def record_for(coating, glass_thickness_mm: float = 4.0, gap_mm: float = 16.0,
85 gas: str = "argon", cost_kwargs: dict | None = None,

What it is
evaluate() built the canonical scoreable record; composition_series() built its own, omitting ten keys — three of them scored criteria.
Why it matters
Harmless only because those three were None for every candidate and the scheme renormalises over what is present. Populate any of them — which is the explicit intent — and the two paths score on different criteria subsets, with nothing to flag it.

Report the sweep, not the ranking

pvdlowe/screening/scoring.py

216def weight_sweep(records, key: str = "Ag_g_per_m2",
217 weights=(0.0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.40),
218 scheme: "ScoringScheme | None" = None) -> "pd.DataFrame":
219 """How the winner changes as one criterion's weight is varied.
220 
221 **Why this exists.** A weight is a value judgement, not a measurement, and
222 some of them decide the answer. Silver mass is the clearest case here: at
223 weight 0 the winner is a 0.065 g/m2 design, at 0.15 it is 0.008, and at
224 0.25 and above it is silver-free. Quoting a single ranking hides that the

What it is
The silver weight of 0.15 is a judgement, not a measurement — and it selects the answer.
Why it matters
At weight 0 the winner carries 0.065 g/m²; at 0.30 it carries none, and the winner changes three times in between. Five of eight settings separate first from second by under half a point.
Do not break
So the honest output is the transitions, not a single ranking. Choosing among them is a decision about how much silver consumption matters to Saint-Gobain — not one the framework can make.
