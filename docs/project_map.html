pvdlowe

No dependencies at all. Physical constants, the evidence-grading enum, and the CIE and solar weighting curves.

The one worth stopping on is provenance.py. Every quantity carries one of eleven evidence grades, and assert_reportable() refuses hypothesis-grade values in headline tables.

The brief itself cautions against putting DFT numbers for a dilute titanium ternary into a thesis as though established. This makes that a type constraint rather than a footnote, so it survives contact with a spreadsheet — and it is what kept those predictions out of the results.

Layer 1 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/materials

How each material responds to light, and how that changes with composition.

2.8×damping discrepancy
53 nmAg mean free path

Lorentz–Drude fits from Rakić et al. (1998), with one deliberate departure that is the most instructive correction in the project.

Rakić's fitted damping for silver implies a DC resistivity of 4.4 µΩ·cm against a true 1.587 — a factor of 2.8 — because in the visible the Drude term trades off against the interband oscillators. Using the published value and a thin-film size-effect multiplier counts the same scattering twice.

That double-count inflated modelled emissivity roughly threefold.

Worth knowingDocumented in METHODOLOGY §2, because anyone building on published optical fits will hit the same trap.

Layer 2 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/electrical

What happens to conduction when the film is thinner than the electron mean free path.

2.79×bulk at 10 nm
8×the copper gap

Full Fuchs–Sondheimer for surface scattering, Mayadas–Shatzkes for grain boundaries, and percolation below a critical thickness.

10 nm2.79× bulk4.44 µΩ·cm
20 nm1.94×3.08
50 nm1.39×2.21
100 nm1.19×1.90

A 10 nm silver film is nearly three times more resistive than bulk. That single fact caught one of the brief's transcribed values — a claim of 1.59 µΩ·cm, which is bulk silver to three figures.

Worth knowingThis is where the framework's largest error lives: it under-predicts sputtered copper by roughly eightfold, and a Ti seed layer recovers most of it.

Layer 3 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/optics

A vectorised transfer-matrix solver, and the standards that turn spectra into numbers.

5decimals on Fresnel
1e-10energy conservation

Arbitrary layers, complex indices, both polarisations, oblique incidence, incoherent thick substrate. It takes arrays rather than scalars, which is why a 168-point composition series with geometry re-optimised at every point finishes in minutes.

How we know it is right is not by inspection. Four checks against closed-form answers:

bare interfaceR = 0.04258Fresnel 0.04258
quarter-waveexact nullat √(n₁n₂)
absorbing stackR+T+A = 1to 1e-10
normal incidences = pidentically

Then EN 410 / ISO 9050 for transmittance, EN 12898 for emissivity against a 283 K Planck radiance, EN 673 for the U-value.

Worth knowingThese tests check the model obeys physical law, not that a function returns the right type.

Layer 4 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/screening

Turning performance into an ordering — and auditing the scheme that does it.

6weighting defects
29%more silver at 100/100

Derringer–Suich desirability, aggregated geometrically so a candidate scoring zero on any criterion goes to zero overall. A weighted sum cannot deliver what the brief asks for; a geometric mean can.

The valuable part is that it audits its own inputs. check-weights found six defects:

emissivity ~ sheet resistancer = 0.978
silver mass ~ metal costr = 1.000
silver mass ~ supply riskr = 0.991

Three criteria that are one physical quantity, carrying 36% of the effective weight between them. And three more carried 30% of the nominal weight while being None for every candidate.

Worth knowingAlso here: record_for(), the single place a scoreable record is built. There were two, differing by ten keys.

Layer 5 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/optimize

Everything below, searched over.

~1,300evaluations per optimisation
0.5 nmhow much silver can be thinned

Nelder–Mead thickness optimisation, composition series with geometry re-optimised at every composition, and the silver-reduction curve.

Re-optimising at every point matters. An early conclusion — an optimum at Ag₆₀Cu₄₀ — was an artifact of three sample points at fixed geometry. At 5% resolution with geometry free, the curve is monotonic and every optimum sits at 0–10 at.% Ag.

And this is where the answer to the brief's actual question is:

10.0 nmcontinuousR_s 4.18
9.5 nmdiscontinuousR_s 5.90

Percolation is the binding constraint, not any of the three specified properties. A 5% thickness reduction costs 41% in sheet resistance.

Worth knowingThree of five overturned conclusions came from reading structure into too few points. This is the module where that happened.

The inputs that decide the answer

data/

Candidate definitions, benchmark transcriptions with verification status, and the two weighting files.

A ranking is meaningless without the weighting file that produced it. The heating and cooling profiles disagree on the winner and on nine of ten top-ten positions. That is not a caveat — it is a property of multi-objective scoring, which is why both files ship.

benchmarks.yaml carries verification state per entry: three verified from full text, four confirmed against abstracts, and two disputed whose figures appear in no locatable source.
