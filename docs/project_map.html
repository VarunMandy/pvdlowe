pvdlowe

No dependencies at all. Physical constants, the evidence-grading enum, and the CIE and solar weighting curves.

The interesting one is provenance.py. Every quantity in the framework carries an evidence grade — MEASURED, LITERATURE, MODEL, HYPOTHESIS and six others — and assert_reportable() refuses to admit hypothesis-grade values into headline tables.

The brief itself cautions against putting DFT numbers for a dilute titanium ternary into a thesis as though they were established. This makes that a type constraint rather than a footnote, so it survives contact with a spreadsheet.

Layer 1 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/materials

How each material responds to light, and how that changes with composition.

2.8×damping discrepancy
53 nmAg mean free path

Lorentz–Drude fits from Rakić et al. (1998), with one deliberate departure that is the most instructive correction in the project.

Rakić's fitted damping for silver implies a DC resistivity of 4.4 µΩ·cm against a true 1.587 — a factor of 2.8 — because in the visible the Drude term trades off against the interband oscillators. Using the published value and a thin-film size-effect multiplier counts the same scattering twice.

That double-count inflated modelled emissivity roughly threefold in an early build.

Worth knowingDocumented in METHODOLOGY §2 because anyone building on published optical fits will hit the same trap.

Layer 2 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/electrical

What happens to conduction when the film is thinner than the electron mean free path.

2.79×bulk at 10 nm
8×the copper gap

The full Fuchs–Sondheimer integral for surface scattering, Mayadas–Shatzkes for grain boundaries, and a percolation model below a critical thickness.

The magnitude is easy to underestimate:

10 nm2.79× bulk4.44 µΩ·cm
20 nm1.94×3.08
50 nm1.39×2.21
100 nm1.19×1.90

A 10 nm silver film is nearly three times more resistive than bulk. Any claim that a thin film reaches bulk resistivity is wrong on its face — which is how one of the brief's transcribed values was caught.

Worth knowingThis is also where the framework's largest known error lives: it under-predicts sputtered copper by roughly eightfold, and grain structure is the leading explanation.

Layer 3 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/optics

A vectorised transfer-matrix solver, and the European standards that turn spectra into numbers.

5decimal places on Fresnel
1e-10energy conservation

Arbitrary layer count, complex indices, both polarisations, oblique incidence, incoherent thick substrate. It takes arrays rather than scalars — a whole spectrum per call — which is why a 168-point composition series with geometry re-optimised at every point finishes in minutes.

How we know it is right is not by inspection. Four checks against closed-form answers:

bare interfaceR = 0.04258Fresnel 0.04258
quarter-waveexact nullat √(n₁n₂)
absorbing stackR+T+A = 1to 1e-10
normal incidences = pidentically

Then integrate.py applies EN 410 / ISO 9050 for transmittance, EN 12898 for emissivity against a 283 K Planck radiance, EN 673 for the U-value.

Worth knowingThese are the tests worth having: they check the model obeys physical law, not that a function returns the right type.

Layer 4 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/screening

Turning performance into an ordering — and auditing the scheme that does it.

6weighting defects found
r=1.000cost is silver, restated

Derringer–Suich desirability with geometric aggregation, so a candidate scoring zero on any criterion goes to zero overall. A weighted sum does not do that, and the brief explicitly asks for behaviour a weighted sum cannot give.

The diagnostics are the valuable part, because they audit their own inputs. check-weights found six defects in the proposed weighting, including:

emissivity ~ sheet resistancer = 0.978
silver mass ~ metal costr = 1.000
silver mass ~ supply riskr = 0.991

Three criteria that are the same physical quantity, carrying 36% of the effective weight between them.

Worth knowingAlso here: record_for(), the single place a scoreable record is built. There were two, differing by ten keys.

Layer 5 · imports only from below

pvdlowe/optimize

Everything below, searched over.

~1,300evaluations per optimisation
0.5 nmhow much silver can be thinned

Nelder–Mead thickness optimisation, composition series with the geometry re-optimised at every composition, and the silver-reduction curve.

Re-optimising at every point matters more than it sounds. An early conclusion — that an optimum existed at Ag₆₀Cu₄₀ — turned out to be an artifact of three sample points at fixed geometry. At 5% resolution with geometry free, the curve is monotonic and every optimum sits at 0–10 at.% Ag.

And this is where the wall is. Thinning the silver in AZO/Ag/AZO:

10.0 nmcontinuousR_s 4.18
9.5 nmdiscontinuousR_s 5.90

Percolation, not any of the three specified properties, is the binding constraint. A 5% thickness reduction costs 41% in sheet resistance.

Worth knowingThree of four overturned conclusions in this project came from reading structure into too few points. This is the module where that happened.

Retrieves convex-hull data for the fifteen chemical systems the brief nominates. Cached, network-gated, and it raises rather than inventing data when offline with a cold cache. It established that Ag–Cu has no stable ordered compound — Cu₃Ag at 0.0904 eV/atom above the hull, CuAg₃ at 0.0857.

pvdlowe/ml

Machine-learning interatomic potentials as a stand-in for DFT. Gated: it must reproduce two known Materials Project hull distances before any prediction is accepted. It produced the Ag–Cu mixing energies, and failed twice at the nucleation question — once on lattice mismatch, once on termination sensitivity. Both failures are documented with the diagnostic that caught them.

The inputs that decide the answer

data/

Candidate definitions, the benchmark transcriptions with their verification status, and the two weighting files.

A ranking from this framework is meaningless without the weighting file that produced it. The heating and cooling profiles disagree on the winner and on nine of ten top-ten positions. That is not a caveat — it is a property of multi-objective scoring, and it is why both files ship.

benchmarks.yaml carries verification state per entry: three fully verified from full text, four confirmed against abstracts, and two disputed whose figures appear in no locatable source.

133 assertions, mostly about physics

tests/

Energy conservation, quarter-wave nulls, the impedance round-trip, the conductive oxide rejecting solar near-infrared. These check that the model obeys physical law.

Plus a guard for every defect that has been fixed, so it fails if one regresses — including one that asserts excluding disputed benchmarks must raise the reported error, because if it lowered it the exclusion would be flattering the model rather than protecting against it.

No pytest. python tests/run_tests.py runs the lot in about ninety seconds.
